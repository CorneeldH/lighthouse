Lighthouse maps

Six maps that show how lighthouse works. Pick one.

Why lighthouse?

Issues are the source of truth. Work starts and ends with a GitHub issue. What the project is doing next lives outside any session or tool: in a place you can read, link to and leave, so you never get locked into one system.

Superpowers is the full set, and not every job needs all of it. The Superpowers skills cover the whole path from a brainstorm to a finished branch. A small job does not need a spec and a plan. route-issue picks how much of the set a job needs: those are the routes on the chain map.

Reviews where mistakes are cheapest. An agent of another model family reviews the spec, the plan and the finished work. In practice this catches real errors before they turn into code. Wes McKinney and Steve Yegge both stress these extra checks as what it takes to run good software with agents.

A factory around the skills. A skill is a strong part, but on its own it knows nothing of the step before or after it. Many setups pick one skill or one framework and stay inside it. Lighthouse connects the parts with handoffs, hooks and gates, and lets you watch how they work together.

Best practice that stays flexible. Superpowers and other skills are used, adapted and updated as they improve. You follow good practice without being tied to one version of it.

Slower for the machine, faster for you. The checks cost agent time. They save your time: less to review, and less to repair afterwards.

Further reading: Wes McKinney, How Kenn is doing Agentic Engineering · the blog of Jesse Vincent, who makes Superpowers · Steve Yegge on the software factory
